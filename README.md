# CRIGram

*Cell by cell, decoding our science.*

**Play:** https://jvdesomp.github.io/crigram/

CRIGram is a free picture logic puzzle (a nonogram) built from figures in research papers of the [Cancer Research Institute Ghent (CRIG)](https://www.crig.ugent.be). Every puzzle hides a detail of a real figure. Solve the grid and the original figure appears, together with a plain-language summary of the research and a link to the paper.

## How to play

- The numbers next to each row and above each column give the lengths of the filled blocks in that line, in order.
- Between two blocks there is at least one empty cell.
- Use **Fill** to colour cells and **Blank** to mark cells that stay empty.
- Every puzzle can be solved by logic alone, without guessing.

A new puzzle appears every Monday at 06:00 (Brussels time). Solve it within the week to keep your streak going.

CRIGram works in any modern browser and can be installed as an app on your phone: open the link and tap **Install app**.

## The science behind the puzzles

Each puzzle shows the authors, the journal, the DOI and the license of the original figure after it is solved. Figures are reused under their open-access license (for example CC BY 4.0) or with permission.

## Privacy

- Your progress and statistics are stored only on your own device, in your browser.
- CRIGram counts anonymous plays with [GoatCounter](https://www.goatcounter.com) to see which puzzles are popular. It uses no cookies and collects no personal data.
- Feedback and sign-ups for CRIG news go through a Microsoft Forms form at Ghent University, and only when you choose to fill it in.

## About

CRIGram was created by Jo Vandesompele (CRIG, Ghent University). The code was written from scratch with the help of Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI assistant by Anthropic. It does not reuse code from other puzzle games. The puzzle type itself, the nonogram, was invented in 1987 by Non Ishida and Tetsuya Nishio.

The puzzles are made with a companion tool, the CRIGram maker, which turns a figure into a grid, checks that the puzzle has exactly one solution that can be found by logic alone, and estimates its difficulty.
