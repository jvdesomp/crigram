# CRIGram

*Cell by cell, decoding our science.*

**Play:** https://www.crig.ugent.be/crigram (in English or Dutch)

CRIGram is a free picture logic puzzle (a nonogram) built from figures in research papers of the [Cancer Research Institute Ghent (CRIG)](https://www.crig.ugent.be). Most puzzles hide a detail of a real figure. Solve the grid and the original figure appears, together with a plain-language summary of the research and a link to the paper. Now and then a puzzle is about CRIG itself, such as an event or an anniversary.

## How to play

- The numbers next to each row and above each column give the lengths of the filled blocks in that line, in order.
- Between two blocks there is at least one empty cell.
- Use **Fill** to color cells and **Blank** to mark cells that stay empty.
- Every puzzle can be solved by logic alone, without guessing. Stuck? The bulb gives a hint.
- There is no clock while you play. Stars show how hard a puzzle is; your time appears once you have solved it.
- New to nonograms? Start with the small practice puzzle.

A new puzzle appears every Monday at 06:00 (Brussels time), or occasionally on special dates. Solve the Monday puzzle within the week to keep your streak going; special puzzles are a bonus. **Weekly reminder** puts a Monday reminder in your calendar. Every figure you uncover is added to your album.

CRIGram works in any modern browser and can be installed as an app on your phone: open the link and tap **Install app**.

## Languages

CRIGram speaks English and Dutch. It follows the language of your browser (on a phone: the language of your phone), and you can switch with **EN · NL** at the top of the home screen or in How to play. A link can ask for Dutch with `?lang=nl`, for example https://www.crig.ugent.be/crigram?lang=nl. The puzzles, your progress and your streak are the same in both languages.

## The science behind the puzzles

Each puzzle shows the authors, the journal and the license of the original figure after it is solved, with a link to the paper. Figures are reused under their open-access license (for example CC BY 4.0) or with permission.

## Privacy

- Your progress, statistics and language choice are stored only on your own device, in your browser.
- CRIGram counts anonymous plays with [GoatCounter](https://www.goatcounter.com) to see which puzzles are popular. It uses no cookies and collects no personal data.
- Feedback goes through a form on [Fillout](https://www.fillout.com) (crigram.zite.so), and only when you choose to fill it in.

## About

CRIGram was created by Jo Vandesompele (CRIG, Ghent University) on 11 October 2026. The code was written from scratch with the help of Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI assistant by Anthropic. It does not reuse code from other puzzle games. The puzzle type itself, the nonogram, was invented in 1987 by Non Ishida and Tetsuya Nishio.

The puzzles are made with a companion tool, the CRIGram maker, which turns a figure into a grid, checks that the puzzle has exactly one solution that can be found by logic alone, and estimates its difficulty.

Why a cancer research institute makes a puzzle game: [Why we turned our cancer research into puzzles](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-we-turned-our-cancer-research-puzzles-jo-vandesompele-wxohe/) (LinkedIn, 17 October 2026).
